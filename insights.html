SOC OPERATIONS

AI Assisted SOC Investigations

What makes a case brief useful, traceable and ready for analyst review?

Read insight

ORYVANTAI PERSPECTIVES

Start with the evidence

An investigation brief should make the evidence easier to inspect. Permitted events, asset context and relevant case history give the analyst a starting point. Source references allow observations to be checked against the original records.

Separate facts from open questions

A useful brief distinguishes observed facts, interpretation and missing context. AI can help align a timeline and prepare next checks, but an unexplained verdict is not a substitute for a reviewable account.

Keep the decision with the analyst

The reviewer verifies the context and decides what happens next. A named reviewer and the relevant approved procedure make responsibility explicit. There should be no silent case closure or unapproved production response.

SIEM & LOGS

Designing Safer SIEM Automation

Why bounded queries, validation and permissions belong in the workflow.

Read insight

ORYVANTAI PERSPECTIVES

Define the question first

A bounded query starts with a clear analyst question and agreed sources. The scope should identify what evidence is needed, who may access it and which system will execute the query.

Validate before execution

Query syntax and access must be validated before execution. Original logs and trusted counts remain available for review. Explanations should make collection and parsing gaps visible rather than hide them.

Review tuning as a change

Reviewed cases can inform a prioritised tuning backlog. Proposed changes need coverage testing and approval before they are accepted. Platform capabilities and integration permissions must be confirmed within the customer scope.

PROCEDURES

Turning Incident Lessons into Better Security Procedures

Connect accepted case experience to company specific SOPs.

Read insight

ORYVANTAI PERSPECTIVES

Begin with a reviewed case

An incident can reveal a delayed handover, an unclear owner or missing evidence. Once the findings are reviewed, those lessons can support a specific procedure proposal.

Make the proposal inspectable

Explain what changes, why it changes and who is responsible. Use approved policies, roles and documentation as the knowledge foundation. Keep assumptions and open questions visible to the procedure owner.

Publish with ownership

The procedure owner decides what is approved and published. Testing, version history and an agreed route back to the previous version make accepted changes easier to manage. AI prepares the proposal; it does not assume publication authority.

THREAT DETECTION

An Anomaly Is a Starting Point, Not a Verdict

Add behaviour and operating context before escalating a signal.

Read insight

ORYVANTAI PERSPECTIVES

Ask what changed

A deviation needs context. Relevant assets, expected activity and reviewed case history help analysts decide which differences deserve further checks within an agreed scenario.

Test the interpretation

An anomaly is not proof of an attack. Detection logic and coverage need review, and explainable operational changes should remain part of the investigation. Context supports a decision rather than replacing it.

SECURITY ANALYTICS

Reporting That Keeps the Evidence in View

Connect technical findings to operational and management decisions.

Read insight

ORYVANTAI PERSPECTIVES

Use validated inputs

Operational reporting should start with validated case data and trusted calculations. Source context helps the reader understand what a finding means and where the evidence is incomplete.

Match the output to the decision

Security teams may need recurring issues and tuning priorities. Leaders may need risk context and the status of accepted improvements. Both views should connect findings to decisions and distinguish accepted changes from unresolved proposals.

CONTROLLED AI

Start with One Security Workflow

Define the evidence, owner and operating boundary before adding assistance.

Read insight

ORYVANTAI PERSPECTIVES

Choose a recurring task

A focused investigation, procedure or reporting task can be a useful starting point. Confirm the evidence, the decision owner, the permitted systems and the expected output before defining the assistance.

Build on a usable foundation

When telemetry, ownership or procedures are incomplete, begin with readiness and engineering. AI assistance should follow a usable evidence foundation. Connect additional workflows only where that improves a real operational task.
